Unit 731 was a covert biological and chemical warfare research unit of the Imperial Japanese Army, established in the mid-1930s and headquartered in the Pingfang district near Harbin in Japanese-occupied Manchuria. Led by army officer and physician Shiro Ishii, it operated under the cover of a water-purification department while conducting weapons research.

The unit carried out lethal experiments on human beings, including deliberate infection with plague, cholera, and other pathogens, as well as other forms of medical experimentation on prisoners. It also developed and field-tested biological weapons, and Japanese forces are documented to have deployed such agents against locations in China during the war, causing further deaths among the civilian population.

After Japan's surrender, many senior members of the program were not prosecuted at the Tokyo Trial; documentary and testimonial evidence indicates that some received immunity from United States authorities in exchange for data from the experiments. The activities of Unit 731 have since been documented through postwar investigations, wartime records, and the testimony of former participants, and they remain a subject of historical research and remembrance.
