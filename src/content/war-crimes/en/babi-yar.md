The Babi Yar massacre was the mass shooting of Jewish civilians at a ravine on the outskirts of Kyiv on 29 and 30 September 1941, shortly after German forces occupied the city. It was carried out by SS and German police units, principally Sonderkommando 4a of Einsatzgruppe C, with the assistance of auxiliary forces.

According to the perpetrators' own operational report, 33,771 Jews were killed over the two days. Victims were ordered to assemble under the pretext of resettlement, marched to the ravine, and shot in groups. In the months and years that followed, Babi Yar was used repeatedly as a killing site for additional victims, including Roma, Soviet prisoners of war, and others, adding to the total number of people murdered there.

Babi Yar became one of the most widely known sites of the mass shootings often called the "Holocaust by bullets," which claimed the lives of Jews across German-occupied Soviet territory. It is documented through German records, survivor accounts, and postwar investigations, and it is commemorated as a major site of Holocaust memory in Ukraine.
