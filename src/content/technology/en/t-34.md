The T-34 was a Soviet medium tank that entered service in 1940 and became one of the most influential armoured fighting vehicles of the Second World War. It combined a powerful 76.2 mm gun, sloped armour that improved protection, and wide tracks that gave good mobility over soft ground and snow.

When Germany invaded the Soviet Union in 1941, the T-34 came as a shock to German forces, outclassing most of their tanks of the period. Designed for mass production, it was relatively simple to build and maintain, allowing Soviet factories to produce it in enormous numbers even after wartime relocation east of the Urals.

Later upgraded to the T-34-85 with an 85 mm gun and a larger turret, the tank remained in production and service throughout the war. Tens of thousands were built, and the T-34 is widely regarded as a decisive factor in Soviet victory on the Eastern Front.
