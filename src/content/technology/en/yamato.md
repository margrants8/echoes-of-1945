The Yamato-class were battleships of the Imperial Japanese Navy, the heaviest and most powerfully armed battleships ever constructed. The lead ship, Yamato, was completed in December 1941, followed by her sister ship Musashi. They displaced around 70,000 tons at full load and mounted nine 46 cm (18.1-inch) main guns, the largest calibre ever fitted to a warship.

The ships were built in great secrecy as Japan sought a qualitative edge to offset the numerical superiority of the United States Navy. However, by the time they entered service, naval air power had become decisive, and the huge battleships spent much of their careers with limited opportunity to use their main armament against enemy warships.

Both ships were ultimately sunk by overwhelming American air attack: Musashi during the Battle of Leyte Gulf in October 1944, and Yamato in April 1945 during a one-way mission toward Okinawa. Their loss came to symbolise the eclipse of the battleship by the aircraft carrier as the dominant capital ship of the war.
