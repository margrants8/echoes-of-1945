The V-2, designated Aggregat 4 (A-4) by its developers, was a German liquid-fuelled ballistic missile and the first man-made object to reach the edge of space. Developed at the Peenemünde research centre under a team including Wernher von Braun, it was powered by an alcohol-and-liquid-oxygen engine and followed a ballistic trajectory to its target.

Deployed operationally from September 1944, the V-2 was used against London, Antwerp, and other Allied cities. Because it arrived faster than the speed of sound, it gave no audible warning before impact, and there was no effective defence against it once launched. It was one of the weapons the Nazi regime promoted as a "Vengeance weapon" (Vergeltungswaffe).

The V-2 caused thousands of deaths among the civilian populations it struck, but its production also cost many thousands of lives among concentration-camp prisoners forced to build it in the underground Mittelwerk factory. After the war, captured V-2 hardware and its engineers were taken to the United States and the Soviet Union, directly influencing post-war rocketry and the space programmes that followed.
