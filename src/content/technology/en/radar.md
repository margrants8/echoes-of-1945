Radar, or radio detection and ranging, uses radio waves to detect the range, direction, and altitude of distant objects such as aircraft and ships. Several nations developed radar in the 1930s, but Britain was among the first to build it into an operational, integrated air-defence network before the Second World War.

Chain Home was the British coastal early-warning radar system, a chain of tall transmitting and receiving towers that could detect incoming aircraft while they were still far out over the sea. Crucially, its information was fed into an integrated command and control network, the Dowding System, which directed fighters to intercept raids and greatly multiplied the effectiveness of the defending force.

During the Battle of Britain in 1940, this radar-based early-warning and control system helped the Royal Air Force concentrate its limited fighters where they were most needed. Radar technology advanced rapidly during the war, including the development of the cavity magnetron, which enabled compact, high-power airborne and shipborne sets.
