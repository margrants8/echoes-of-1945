The Messerschmitt Bf 109 was a German single-seat fighter designed by Willy Messerschmitt and first flown in 1935, entering Luftwaffe service in 1937. It was among the first modern fighters of its generation, featuring an all-metal monocoque construction, a retractable undercarriage, and an enclosed cockpit.

The Bf 109 served as the backbone of the German fighter force throughout the war, seeing combat from the Spanish Civil War through to 1945. Powered by Daimler-Benz inverted V-12 engines in most variants, it was continually upgraded and armed with a mix of machine guns and cannon.

With over 30,000 aircraft produced, the Bf 109 was one of the most-built fighter aircraft in history. It was flown by many of the Luftwaffe's leading aces and remained in front-line service until the end of the war in Europe.
