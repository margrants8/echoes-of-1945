The Supermarine Spitfire was a British single-seat fighter designed by R. J. Mitchell and first flown in 1936, entering Royal Air Force service in 1938. Its distinctive elliptical wing and Rolls-Royce Merlin engine gave it high speed and excellent manoeuvrability, making it one of the most recognisable aircraft of the Second World War.

Alongside the Hawker Hurricane, the Spitfire played a central role in the Battle of Britain in 1940, defending the United Kingdom against the German Luftwaffe. Continuous development through numerous marks kept it competitive throughout the war, with later versions powered by the more powerful Rolls-Royce Griffon engine.

More than 20,000 Spitfires were built, and the type served in every major theatre of the war and in the air forces of several Allied nations. It remained in production longer than any other British fighter of the period and became an enduring symbol of British resistance.
