Douglas MacArthur was a United States Army general who played a central role in the Pacific theater of the Second World War. The son of a senior Army officer, he graduated from West Point and served with distinction in the First World War before commanding forces in the Philippines.

After Japanese forces overran the Philippines in early 1942, MacArthur was ordered to Australia, famously pledging "I shall return." He directed the Allied campaign that advanced through New Guinea and returned to the Philippines with the landings on Leyte in October 1944. He was promoted to General of the Army in December 1944.

On September 2, 1945, MacArthur accepted the formal surrender of Japan aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay. As Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, he oversaw the occupation and reconstruction of Japan until 1951. He later commanded United Nations forces in the early stages of the Korean War before being relieved of command by President Harry Truman in 1951.
