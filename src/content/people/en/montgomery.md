Bernard Law Montgomery, 1st Viscount Montgomery of Alamein, was one of the most prominent British commanders of the Second World War. A veteran of the First World War, he rose to command the British Eighth Army in North Africa in 1942.

Montgomery is best known for his victory at the Second Battle of El Alamein in October and November 1942, which halted the Axis advance in Egypt and marked a turning point in the North African campaign. His careful, methodical approach to battle and his emphasis on morale made him a popular figure with the troops, though his self-assured manner sometimes strained relations with Allied colleagues.

In 1944 he served as commander of the 21st Army Group and led Allied ground forces during the initial phase of the Normandy invasion. His plan for a rapid advance into the Netherlands, Operation Market Garden, ended in costly failure in September 1944. After the war Montgomery held senior NATO posts and was raised to the peerage as a viscount.
