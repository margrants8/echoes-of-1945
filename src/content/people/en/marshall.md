George C. Marshall was a career U.S. Army officer who served as Army Chief of Staff throughout the Second World War, from 1939 to 1945. In that role he oversaw the vast expansion of the U.S. Army and directed American strategy in cooperation with Allied leaders, earning a reputation as the principal architect of the American war effort.

Marshall played a central part in inter-Allied planning at wartime conferences and helped shape the decision to invade German-occupied France. He recommended Dwight D. Eisenhower to command Operation Overlord, the Normandy invasion of June 1944. He was promoted to General of the Army in December 1944.

After the war Marshall served as Secretary of State (1947-1949) and Secretary of Defense (1950-1951). As Secretary of State he lent his name to the European Recovery Program, widely known as the Marshall Plan, for which he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1953. He died in Washington, D.C., in October 1959.
