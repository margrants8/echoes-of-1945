Josip Broz, known as Tito, was a Yugoslav communist revolutionary who led the Partisan resistance movement against Axis occupation during the Second World War. Following the invasion and partition of Yugoslavia by Germany and its allies in 1941, he organized an armed uprising and became commander of the People's Liberation Army.

Under Tito's leadership, the Partisans grew into one of the most effective resistance movements in occupied Europe, waging a prolonged guerrilla campaign against German, Italian, and collaborationist forces. By 1943 the Allies had shifted their support to Tito's movement, and his forces played a major role in liberating the country, entering Belgrade in 1944 with Soviet assistance.

After the war Tito became the leader of a reconstituted socialist Yugoslavia. In 1948 he broke with Joseph Stalin, steering an independent course, and he later became a prominent figure in the Non-Aligned Movement. He governed Yugoslavia until his death in May 1980.
