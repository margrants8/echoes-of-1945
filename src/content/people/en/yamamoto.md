Isoroku Yamamoto was a Japanese naval officer who served as commander-in-chief of the Imperial Japanese Navy's Combined Fleet from 1939. Having studied and served in the United States earlier in his career, he was familiar with American industrial capacity and had expressed reservations about the prospect of a prolonged war with the United States.

Once war was decided upon, Yamamoto planned and directed the surprise attack on the U.S. Pacific Fleet at Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941. In 1942 he oversaw further offensive operations, including the plan that led to the Battle of Midway in June, a decisive defeat for the Japanese navy in which it lost four aircraft carriers.

Yamamoto was killed on April 18, 1943, when the aircraft carrying him was shot down over Bougainville in the Solomon Islands by U.S. fighter planes, in an operation launched after American forces intercepted and decoded his travel itinerary. He was posthumously promoted to Marshal Admiral and remains one of the most studied naval commanders of the Pacific War.
