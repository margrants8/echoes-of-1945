Joseph Stalin was the leader of the Soviet Union from the mid-1920s until his death in 1953, holding the position of General Secretary of the Communist Party and later Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars. During the Second World War, the Soviet Union was one of the principal Allied powers.

After Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, Stalin directed the Soviet war effort as head of the State Defense Committee and Supreme Commander. The Eastern Front saw some of the largest and most costly battles of the war, including Moscow, Stalingrad, and Kursk, and the Soviet Union sustained enormous military and civilian casualties before reaching Berlin in 1945.

Stalin took part in the Allied conferences at Tehran, Yalta, and Potsdam, where the postwar order in Europe was negotiated. His rule is also associated with extensive political repression and forced collectivization, subjects documented across a large body of historical scholarship.
