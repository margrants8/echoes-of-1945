Georgy Zhukov was a Marshal of the Soviet Union and one of the most prominent Soviet military commanders of the Second World War. He rose to senior command on the strength of his performance at the Battle of Khalkhin Gol against Japanese forces in 1939 and his subsequent role as Chief of the General Staff.

Zhukov played a leading part in many of the decisive engagements on the Eastern Front. He was central to the defense of Moscow in the winter of 1941, coordinated Soviet forces at Stalingrad and Kursk, and commanded the 1st Belorussian Front during the final advance into Germany. In May 1945 he accepted the German unconditional surrender on behalf of the Soviet high command in Berlin.

After the war Zhukov's public prominence fluctuated with Soviet politics; he served as Minister of Defense in the mid-1950s before being removed from the leadership. He remains widely regarded as one of the leading commanders of the conflict.
