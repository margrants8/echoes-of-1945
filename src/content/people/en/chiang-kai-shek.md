Chiang Kai-shek was the leader of the Republic of China and the commander-in-chief of its armed forces during the Second World War. As head of the Nationalist government and the Kuomintang party, he led China's resistance to the Japanese invasion that began with full-scale war in 1937.

After the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941 brought the United States into the Pacific War, China became one of the principal Allied powers. Chiang was recognized as the Allied supreme commander in the China theater, and he met with Franklin D. Roosevelt and Winston Churchill at the Cairo Conference in 1943, where the Allies affirmed war aims concerning Japanese-occupied territory.

Following Japan's defeat in 1945, the long-running conflict between Chiang's Nationalists and the Chinese Communists resumed as full civil war. Defeated on the mainland in 1949, Chiang relocated his government to Taiwan, where he continued to lead the Republic of China until his death in April 1975.
