Harry S. Truman was the 33rd President of the United States, serving from 1945 to 1953. A Democrat from Missouri, he had been a U.S. senator and briefly vice president before the death of Franklin D. Roosevelt on April 12, 1945, elevated him to the presidency less than four months into the term.

Truman led the United States through the final months of the Second World War. In the summer of 1945 he attended the Potsdam Conference with Winston Churchill (later Clement Attlee) and Joseph Stalin, and he authorized the use of atomic bombs against the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. Japan announced its surrender shortly afterward, ending the war.

In the postwar years Truman shaped the early Cold War through policies such as the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan for European recovery, and he oversaw U.S. involvement in the founding of the United Nations and NATO. He was elected to a full term in 1948 and left office in 1953. He died in Kansas City, Missouri, in December 1972.
