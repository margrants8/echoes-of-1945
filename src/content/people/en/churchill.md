Winston Churchill served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1940 to 1945, leading Britain through most of the Second World War. He took office in May 1940 as German forces advanced across Western Europe, and his refusal to seek terms with Nazi Germany became a defining feature of British wartime policy.

Churchill's wartime speeches, delivered during the Battle of Britain and the Blitz, are widely credited with sustaining public morale during a period of severe strain. He worked closely with U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt and Soviet leader Joseph Stalin, forming the core of the Allied coalition and taking part in the major wartime conferences at Tehran, Yalta, and Potsdam.

Although his Conservative Party lost the general election in July 1945, Churchill remained a central figure in postwar politics and served a second term as Prime Minister from 1951 to 1955. He was also an accomplished writer and was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1953.
