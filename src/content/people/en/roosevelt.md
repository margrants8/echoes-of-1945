Franklin D. Roosevelt was the 32nd President of the United States, serving from 1933 until his death in April 1945. The only U.S. president elected to four terms, he led the country through both the Great Depression and most of its involvement in the Second World War.

Before the United States formally entered the war, Roosevelt supported the Allied powers through measures such as the Lend-Lease program, which supplied material aid to Britain, the Soviet Union, and other nations. Following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, he asked Congress for a declaration of war, bringing the United States fully into the conflict.

As Commander-in-Chief, Roosevelt coordinated wartime strategy with Winston Churchill and Joseph Stalin, taking part in the conferences at Tehran and Yalta. He died of a cerebral hemorrhage on April 12, 1945, less than a month before the German surrender in Europe, and was succeeded by Vice President Harry S. Truman.
