Erwin Rommel was a German army officer who rose to the rank of Field Marshal during the Second World War. He first gained prominence commanding a panzer division during the 1940 invasion of France, and is most closely associated with the North African campaign, where his leadership of the Afrika Korps earned him the nickname "the Desert Fox."

Rommel's forces achieved a series of advances across North Africa in 1941 and 1942 before being decisively defeated at the Second Battle of El Alamein and forced into retreat. In 1944 he was assigned to strengthen the German coastal defenses in northern France ahead of the anticipated Allied invasion, and he commanded army group forces during the Normandy fighting.

Rommel's later relationship with the Nazi regime became a subject of historical debate. Implicated in the July 1944 plot against Adolf Hitler, he was compelled to take his own life in October 1944 in exchange for the regime's protection of his family, and his death was publicly presented as the result of wounds. He remains a widely studied but contested figure of the war.
