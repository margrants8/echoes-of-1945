Charles de Gaulle was a French general and statesman who led the Free French Forces during the Second World War. After France's defeat by Germany in June 1940, he rejected the armistice signed by the Vichy government and, from London, broadcast an appeal to continue resistance, becoming the leader of the Free French movement.

Throughout the war de Gaulle worked to unite French resistance groups and to secure recognition for Free France among the Allied powers, at times amid friction with Britain and the United States. Following the Normandy landings and the liberation of Paris in August 1944, he headed the Provisional Government of the French Republic.

De Gaulle resigned from the provisional government in 1946, then returned to public life during the crisis of 1958, founding the Fifth Republic and serving as President of France from 1959 to 1969. He remains a central figure in modern French history.
