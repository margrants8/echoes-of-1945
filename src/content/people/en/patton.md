George Smith Patton Jr. was a United States Army general known for his aggressive leadership of armored forces in the European theater of the Second World War. A career officer who had competed in the 1912 Olympics and served in the First World War, Patton became one of the foremost American practitioners of mobile, mechanized warfare.

In 1943 he commanded American forces in the invasions of North Africa and Sicily. Two incidents in which he struck hospitalized soldiers in Sicily led to a public controversy and a temporary sidelining from command. He returned to lead the Third Army after the Normandy invasion, driving rapidly across France in the summer of 1944.

During the Battle of the Bulge in the winter of 1944–1945, Patton turned his army north to relieve the besieged town of Bastogne, a swift maneuver widely regarded as one of his most notable achievements. He died in December 1945 from injuries sustained in a car accident in occupied Germany.
