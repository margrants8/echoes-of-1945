On October 24, 1945, the United Nations Charter entered into force and the United Nations was officially established. This date is now observed annually as United Nations Day.

Founded by 51 original member states, the UN is headquartered in New York City. Its principal organs include the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Secretariat, and the International Court of Justice. The five permanent members of the Security Council — the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, France, and China — each hold veto power, a structure that reflected the power dynamics of the victorious Allied nations.

The founding of the United Nations marked humanity's first truly universal international organization, established to maintain international peace and security, promote international cooperation, and protect human rights. Though its limitations became apparent during the Cold War, the UN endures as the cornerstone of the post-war international order.
