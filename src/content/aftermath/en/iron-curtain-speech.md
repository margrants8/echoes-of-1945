On March 5, 1946, the former British Prime Minister Winston Churchill delivered an address titled "The Sinews of Peace" at Westminster College in Fulton, Missouri, with President Harry S. Truman seated beside him. Speaking as a private citizen, Churchill used the occasion to warn of the growing division of Europe and the expansion of Soviet influence over the nations of the continent's east.

The speech is best remembered for a single vivid image: "From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the Continent." Behind that line, Churchill argued, lay the ancient capitals of Central and Eastern Europe, increasingly subject to control from Moscow. He called for a close association among the English-speaking peoples to preserve peace and resist the spread of tyranny.

Though Churchill did not coin the phrase "iron curtain," his address fixed it in the public mind as the defining metaphor for the Cold War divide. The speech drew criticism at the time from those who feared it would deepen East-West hostility, but it is now regarded as one of the early rhetorical markers of the emerging Cold War.
