In late May 1940, the German advance through France and Belgium trapped the British Expeditionary Force and substantial French and Belgian forces against the English Channel around the port of Dunkirk. With encirclement threatening, the Admiralty launched Operation Dynamo on 26 May to evacuate the troops by sea.

Over nine days, a fleet of naval vessels supported by hundreds of civilian "little ships" ferried men from the beaches and harbour under continuous Luftwaffe attack. By 4 June, roughly 338,000 Allied soldiers had been carried to England — far more than the few tens of thousands originally thought possible.

The evacuation left behind vast quantities of vehicles, artillery, and equipment, and it did not halt the fall of France, which surrendered later that month. Yet the rescue of the bulk of Britain's field army preserved a trained force for the war ahead and became a lasting emblem of national resolve.
