The Battle of Midway, fought from June 4 to 7, 1942, was a decisive naval engagement between the United States and Imperial Japan in the central Pacific. Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto sought to lure the American carrier fleet into a trap near Midway Atoll and destroy it, but U.S. codebreakers had penetrated Japanese naval communications and forewarned Admiral Chester Nimitz of the plan.

Alerted to the enemy's approach, American carriers ambushed the Japanese striking force. Within a few devastating minutes on the morning of June 4, dive bombers from the USS Enterprise and USS Yorktown fatally crippled three Japanese fleet carriers; a fourth was sunk later that day. Japan lost all four carriers committed to the operation along with their aircraft and many irreplaceable veteran aircrew, while the United States lost the carrier Yorktown and a destroyer.

The battle halted Japanese expansion in the Pacific and shifted the strategic initiative to the United States barely six months after Pearl Harbor. It is widely regarded as the turning point of the Pacific War, after which Japan was increasingly forced onto the defensive.
