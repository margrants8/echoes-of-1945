On 16 April 1945, Soviet forces opened the final assault on Berlin, attacking from bridgeheads on the Oder and Neisse rivers with overwhelming numbers of troops, artillery, and armour. After breaking through the defences at the Seelow Heights, Red Army forces encircled the German capital within days.

Fighting reached the city itself in the last week of April, as Soviet troops advanced street by street toward the government district against a defence assembled from depleted army units, Waffen-SS, police, and Volkssturm militia that included the elderly and the young. Civilians endured heavy bombardment throughout.

Adolf Hitler took his own life in the Führerbunker on 30 April. The city's defenders surrendered on 2 May 1945. The fall of Berlin brought the fighting in Europe to its close, and Germany's unconditional surrender followed within days, ending the war on the continent.
