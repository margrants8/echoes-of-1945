On the morning of December 7, 1941, aircraft of the Imperial Japanese Navy launched a surprise attack on the United States Pacific Fleet anchored at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii. Two waves of carrier-based aircraft struck the naval base and nearby airfields, sinking or damaging much of the fleet and destroying scores of aircraft on the ground.

The attack killed roughly 2,400 Americans and wounded more than a thousand. Eight U.S. battleships were sunk or damaged, among them the USS Arizona, which exploded and remains a memorial to this day. Crucially, the fleet's aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped harm, and the base's repair facilities and fuel reserves survived largely intact.

The following day the United States declared war on Japan, and within days Germany and Italy declared war on the United States. The attack ended American neutrality and brought the country fully into the Second World War, transforming a series of regional conflicts into a truly global war.
