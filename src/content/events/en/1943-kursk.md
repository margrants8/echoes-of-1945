In July 1943, Germany launched Operation Citadel against the Kursk salient, a large Soviet-held bulge in the front line. The plan called for pincer attacks from north and south to encircle the Red Army forces within it. Forewarned of the offensive, Soviet commanders had built deep defensive belts of minefields, trenches, and anti-tank positions.

The German attack ground forward slowly against these defences at heavy cost, particularly in the south, where intense armoured fighting took place near Prokhorovka. The northern pincer made only limited progress. With his offensive stalling and an Allied landing underway in Sicily, Hitler called off Citadel in mid-July.

The Red Army then launched its own counteroffensives, retaking Orel, Belgorod, and Kharkov by late August. Kursk exhausted Germany's armoured reserves and confirmed that the Soviet Union held the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front for the remainder of the war.
