Nazi Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, launching the campaign that is generally taken to mark the beginning of the Second World War. German forces attacked from the north, west, and south, employing fast-moving armored columns supported by aircraft in the pattern later known as Blitzkrieg. Britain and France, bound by treaty to Poland, declared war on Germany on 3 September.

On 17 September, in accordance with the secret protocol of the German–Soviet Nonaggression Pact signed the previous month, the Soviet Union invaded Poland from the east. Caught between two powers and receiving no direct military aid from its Western allies, the Polish army was overwhelmed despite determined resistance, including the prolonged defense of Warsaw, which capitulated on 28 September.

Organized fighting ended with the surrender of the last major Polish field units in early October. Poland was partitioned between Germany and the Soviet Union, and both occupiers imposed harsh regimes. Casualty estimates vary and are complicated by the two-front nature of the campaign, but Polish military and civilian losses ran into the tens of thousands killed, with hundreds of thousands taken prisoner.
