The Second Battle of El Alamein began on October 23, 1942, when the British Eighth Army under General Bernard Montgomery launched a massive offensive against the German–Italian Panzer Army in the Egyptian desert. Preceded by a thousand-gun artillery barrage, the assault sought to break the Axis line at a narrow front bounded by the Mediterranean coast and the impassable Qattara Depression.

Over nearly three weeks of attritional fighting, Commonwealth forces ground down the outnumbered and poorly supplied army commanded by Field Marshal Erwin Rommel. Superior numbers, secure supply lines, and command of the air gradually told against the Axis, whose armour and fuel reserves were steadily depleted.

By November 11 the Axis army was in full retreat westward across Libya, a withdrawal from which it never recovered in North Africa. Coming days before the Allied landings in Morocco and Algeria, the victory marked a decisive turn in the desert war and was celebrated in Britain as the first great land triumph over Germany.
