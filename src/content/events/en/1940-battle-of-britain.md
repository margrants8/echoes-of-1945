Following the fall of France, Nazi Germany sought air superiority over the English Channel and southern England as a precondition for a planned cross-Channel invasion. From July 1940, the Luftwaffe attacked shipping, airfields, radar stations, and eventually cities, while RAF Fighter Command defended with Hurricane and Spitfire squadrons.

Fighter Command's integrated system of radar, ground control, and rapid squadron dispatch allowed British defenders to concentrate against incoming raids. When the Luftwaffe shifted to bombing London and other cities in September, it relieved pressure on the airfields at a critical moment. Aircrew losses on both sides ran into the thousands.

Unable to defeat the RAF or protect an invasion fleet, Germany indefinitely postponed the landing. The Battle of Britain was the first major campaign fought entirely in the air and the first significant defeat for German forces, marking a turning point early in the war.
