The Battle of the Bulge was Germany's last major offensive on the Western Front, launched by surprise on 16 December 1944 through the forested Ardennes region of Belgium and Luxembourg. Hitler hoped to split the Allied armies, drive to the port of Antwerp, and force a negotiated settlement in the west.

Attacking in poor winter weather that grounded Allied aircraft, German forces punched a large bulge into the American line, giving the battle its name. Isolated units mounted stubborn defenses, most famously at the crossroads town of Bastogne, where encircled American troops held out until relieved by elements of Patton's Third Army. As the skies cleared, Allied air power and reinforcements blunted the advance.

The Germans were halted short of the Meuse and gradually pushed back, with the front largely restored by late January 1945. It was the largest and costliest single battle fought by the United States Army in the war; American casualties are commonly estimated at around 75,000 or more, including roughly 19,000 killed, while the offensive exhausted irreplaceable German reserves.
