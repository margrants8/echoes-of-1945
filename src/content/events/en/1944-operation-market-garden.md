Operation Market Garden was an ambitious Allied offensive launched on 17 September 1944 in the German-occupied Netherlands. Conceived by Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery, it combined a large airborne landing (Market) with a ground advance (Garden) intended to seize a series of bridges over the major rivers, opening a route into northern Germany and potentially shortening the war.

American, British, and Polish airborne troops dropped to capture bridges at Eindhoven, Nijmegen, and Arnhem, while British armored forces pushed up a single narrow road to link them. The plan depended on rapid success at every crossing, but stiffening German resistance, difficult terrain, and delays left the British 1st Airborne Division isolated at the furthest bridge at Arnhem.

The ground advance failed to reach Arnhem in time, and the airborne troops there were overwhelmed. Of roughly ten thousand men committed at Arnhem, only around a quarter were withdrawn; the rest were killed or captured. The operation secured a salient but fell short of its central objective, and the northern Netherlands remained under occupation into 1945.
