In October 1941, Germany launched Operation Typhoon, the drive to capture Moscow before winter. German panzer forces encircled large Soviet formations at Vyazma and Bryansk and pressed toward the capital, at some points reaching its outer suburbs. The Soviet government evacuated many institutions eastward, though Stalin remained in the city.

Stiffening Red Army resistance, autumn mud, and the onset of a severe winter slowed the exhausted German advance. Reinforced with fresh divisions, including forces transferred from the Soviet Far East, the defenders held the line as the offensive lost momentum in early December.

On 5–6 December 1941, the Red Army opened a broad counteroffensive that drove German forces back from the approaches to Moscow, in places by more than a hundred kilometres. It was the first major strategic setback for the German army in the war and ended any prospect of a quick German victory in the East.
