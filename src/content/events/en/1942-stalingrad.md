The Battle of Stalingrad began in the summer of 1942 as the German Sixth Army, part of a wider drive toward the Caucasus oil fields, pushed into the city on the Volga. A massive Luftwaffe bombardment in late August devastated the city, and the fighting soon devolved into savage close-quarters combat among ruined factories and buildings.

Soviet forces clung to narrow footholds along the river while reinforcements crossed under fire. On 19 November 1942, the Red Army launched Operation Uranus, a two-pronged offensive that broke through weaker Axis flanks and encircled roughly a quarter of a million German and allied troops within days.

Denied resupply and forbidden to break out, the trapped Sixth Army was ground down through the winter and surrendered in early February 1943. Casualties on both sides, military and civilian, were immense. Stalingrad is widely regarded as the decisive turning point of the war in Europe, after which the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front passed permanently to the Soviet Union.
