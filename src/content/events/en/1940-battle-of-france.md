The Battle of France began on 10 May 1940 when German forces launched simultaneous offensives against France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. While Allied attention was drawn north into Belgium, the German main effort drove armored divisions through the wooded Ardennes, a sector the French high command had considered largely impassable to tanks.

German panzers crossed the Meuse at Sedan and broke into open country, racing to the English Channel and cutting off the best Allied armies in the north. This trapped force was progressively compressed, leading to the evacuation of much of the British Expeditionary Force and French troops from Dunkirk in late May and early June. The Germans then turned south against the remaining French armies.

Paris fell on 14 June, and the French government sought terms. An armistice was signed on 22 June and took effect on 25 June, dividing France into a German-occupied zone and a nominally independent state based at Vichy. The swift defeat of a major power stunned observers worldwide; losses on all sides were heavy, and roughly 1.8 million French soldiers passed into German captivity.
