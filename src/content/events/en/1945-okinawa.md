The Battle of Okinawa began on April 1, 1945, when American forces landed on the largest of the Ryukyu Islands, some 340 miles from the Japanese mainland. Intended as a staging ground for the anticipated invasion of Japan itself, Okinawa became the largest amphibious assault of the Pacific War and one of its most ferocious battles.

Japanese defenders, dug into fortified lines across the island's southern hills, waged a determined defense designed to inflict maximum casualties. At sea, waves of kamikaze aircraft assailed the Allied fleet, sinking dozens of ships and killing thousands of sailors. Torrential rain, mud, and close-quarters fighting made the campaign a grinding ordeal lasting nearly three months.

The battle was catastrophic in human terms. American forces suffered more than 49,000 casualties, including about 12,500 dead; the great majority of the Japanese garrison perished. Okinawan civilians suffered appallingly, with well over 100,000 killed in the crossfire, by shelling, and in mass deaths. The scale of the losses influenced Allied deliberations over how to bring the war against Japan to an end. Organized resistance ended on June 22, 1945.
