On 22 June 1941, Germany and its Axis partners launched Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union, along a front stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea. It was the largest land invasion in history, committing more than three million Axis troops across three army groups aimed at Leningrad, Moscow, and the resource-rich south.

In the opening months, German forces advanced hundreds of kilometres, encircling and capturing enormous numbers of Soviet troops in battles around Minsk, Smolensk, and Kyiv. Despite catastrophic early losses, the Red Army traded space for time, and Soviet industry was relocated eastward to continue arms production.

By December 1941, the offensive had stalled short of Moscow amid lengthening supply lines, mounting casualties, and the onset of a severe winter. Barbarossa's failure to achieve a swift, decisive victory committed Germany to a prolonged war of attrition on the Eastern Front that it ultimately could not sustain.
