The Guadalcanal Campaign opened on August 7, 1942, when U.S. Marines landed on Guadalcanal and neighboring islands in the Solomons to seize an airfield the Japanese were building there. Renamed Henderson Field, the airstrip became the focus of six months of grinding combat on land, at sea, and in the air as both sides fought to control it and the surrounding waters.

The campaign was a prolonged battle of attrition. A series of fierce naval engagements in the surrounding sound — so many ships were lost that it became known as Ironbottom Sound — accompanied repeated Japanese ground offensives against the American perimeter. Tropical disease, hunger, and the difficulty of resupply took a heavy toll, particularly on Japanese forces, whose reinforcement runs down "the Slot" could not keep pace with their losses.

By February 1943 Japan abandoned the island and evacuated its surviving troops. Guadalcanal was the first major Allied ground offensive against Japan and, together with Midway, marked the point at which the strategic initiative in the Pacific passed permanently to the Allies.
