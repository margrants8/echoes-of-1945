The Tehran Conference, held from November 28 to December 1, 1943, was the first wartime meeting of the "Big Three" — U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt, British Prime Minister Winston Churchill, and Soviet leader Joseph Stalin.

The central decision was the timing of the long-awaited second front in Western Europe. Roosevelt and Churchill committed to Operation Overlord, the cross-Channel invasion of France, for May 1944 (it was ultimately launched on June 6). In return, Stalin promised that the Soviet Union would declare war on Japan once Germany was defeated.

The leaders also discussed the shape of post-war Europe, including the contentious question of Poland's borders. Tehran cemented the coordination of Allied strategy on the eastern and western fronts and set the stage for the Yalta and Potsdam conferences that would define the post-war order.
