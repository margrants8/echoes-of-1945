The Cairo Conference was a meeting held in Cairo, Egypt, from November 22 to 26, 1943, bringing together President Franklin D. Roosevelt, Prime Minister Winston Churchill, and Generalissimo Chiang Kai-shek, leader of the Republic of China. Unlike the concurrent talks at Tehran, which focused on the war in Europe, Cairo concentrated on the war against Japan and the future of East Asia.

The three leaders coordinated strategy for the Pacific and the China-Burma-India theater and agreed on their aims for the postwar settlement with Japan. The resulting Cairo Declaration, released on December 1, 1943, stated that the Allies coveted no territorial expansion for themselves but were determined to strip Japan of the territories it had seized since the First World War.

The declaration specified that Manchuria, Formosa (Taiwan), and the Pescadores islands should be restored to China, and it pledged that Korea would in due course become free and independent. These commitments shaped Allied policy toward the postwar order in Asia and were later reaffirmed in the Potsdam Declaration of 1945.
